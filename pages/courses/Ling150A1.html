Language in the World

Every human community has language. This course introduces the concepts and methods of linguistics — the scientific study of language — alongside tools from psychology, anthropology, biology, computation, and philosophy.

About This Course

We use language not only to communicate with each other, but in our dreams and our art — some have even argued that language is the stuff of thought itself. This course introduces concepts and methods in linguistics along with important tools from psychology, anthropology, biology, computation, and philosophy. Students will investigate the diversity of the world’s languages around the principle of functional equivalence, explore the languages and communities indigenous to the Tucson basin, and examine language endangerment, reclamation, and revitalization.

Along the way, students will strengthen their skills in reading for understanding, writing for a scholarly audience, integrating creativity with analysis in a multi-step project, presenting complex information clearly, applying a citation style accurately, responding to feedback, and managing a long-term project against deadlines.

There are no exams in this course — all evaluation is coursework. Discussion posts split evenly between weekly topics and assigned readings (5 of each, 10 total). Assignments are creative, project-based work spread across the five weeks.
