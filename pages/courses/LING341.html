Language Development

How children build a language from nothing — sound, word, and sentence — and the methods used to study minds too young to speak for themselves.

↓ Download Syllabus
About This Course

This is an upper-division course on language development offered by the Linguistics Department, open as an elective to students in Linguistics, Neuroscience & Cognitive Science, Psychology, and Speech, Language, & Hearing Sciences. The course is organized around one question: how do children learn language? We’ll examine what children know at each stage, when and how that knowledge appears, and the theories proposed to explain it — along with the methods researchers use to test children, and even infants, who cannot yet speak for themselves.

By the end of the course you will be able to explain how children map sound onto meaning, how they combine word-parts into words and words into sentences, the link between brain and language, the challenges faced by blind, deaf, or language-delayed children, and how children acquire a second or third language.
